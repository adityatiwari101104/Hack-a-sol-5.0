# Competitive Landscape: Regulatory / Legal / Compliance Document AI vs "Proof-or-Silence"

Scope: commercial SaaS that overlaps a citation-verified, abstaining, contradiction-checking, amendment-aware Q&A system over RBI circulars/master directions plus a bank's own policies. As of November 2026. Labels used throughout: **[VENDOR CLAIM]** = from vendor/press-release/advertorial; **[INDEPENDENT]** = third-party evaluation or reputable press; **[ESTIMATE]** = third-party estimate (pricing aggregators, secondary-market trackers).

## 1. Global legal/compliance AI: citation, hallucination and verification claims vs independent evaluations

### Takeaway
Every major legal AI vendor markets "linked" or "verified" citations, and LexisNexis has used the phrase "hallucination-free", but the only peer-reviewed independent test (Stanford RegLab/HAI, published in JELS 2025) found the leading RAG legal research tools still hallucinated on 17–34% of queries, including "misgrounded" answers where a real citation does not support the claim. The industry benchmark (Vals VLAIR) shows high accuracy on document Q&A (Harvey 94.8%) but the incumbents (Thomson Reuters, LexisNexis) opted out of the 2025 legal-research round, so there is no common, independent benchmark for "abstain when unprovable" behaviour.

### Cited Findings
**Independent evaluations**
- [INDEPENDENT] Stanford RegLab/HAI "Hallucination-Free?" study: Lexis+ AI and Ask Practical Law AI produced incorrect information >17% of the time; Westlaw AI-Assisted Research hallucinated >34% of the time — [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-queries); [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20362v1)
- [INDEPENDENT] Same study: Lexis+ AI answered 65% of queries accurately; Westlaw AI-Assisted Research 42% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20362v1)
- [INDEPENDENT] The study defines two failure modes: (a) incorrect statement of law, and (b) "misgrounded" — a correct-looking claim backed by a citation that does not support it. RAG reduces but does not eliminate hallucination vs GPT-4; vendors' "hallucination-free" claims were judged unsupported — [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-queries); final version published in Journal of Empirical Legal Studies 2025 — [Magesh et al. PDF](https://nlp.stanford.edu/~manning/papers/Magesh-Hallucination%E2%80%90Free-2025.pdf)
- [INDEPENDENT] Methodology dispute: critics said the first preprint tested Thomson Reuters' Practical Law tool instead of Westlaw; TR then gave access to Westlaw AI-Assisted Research and the authors augmented the paper — [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2024/05/stanford-will-augment-its-study-finding-that-ai-legal-research-tools-hallucinate-in-17-of-queries-as-some-raise-questions-about-the-results/)
- [INDEPENDENT] Vals Legal AI Report (VLAIR), Feb 2025: Harvey Assistant top score in 5 of 6 tasks it entered, 94.8% on document Q&A; CoCounsel top on document summarization (77.2%); Lexis+ AI withdrew from the study — [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/02/legal-ai-tools-show-promise-in-first-of-its-kind-benchmark-study-with-harvey-and-cocounsel-leading-the-pack.html); [Legal Technology](https://legaltechnology.com/harvey-and-cocounsel-receive-top-scores-in-first-major-industry-genai-benchmarking-study/); [Vals report page](https://vals.ai/vlair)
- [INDEPENDENT, vendor-hosted page] Vals CoCounsel page lists 89.6% on document Q&A vs lawyer baseline 70.1%; 73.2% on data extraction vs 71.1% for lawyers — [Vals CoCounsel](https://www.vals.ai/application-reports/cocounsel)
- [INDEPENDENT] Vals VLAIR Legal Research (report dated 14 Oct 2025): 210 questions across nine research types; accuracy — lawyers 71%, Alexi 80%, Counsel Stack 81%, Midpage 79%, ChatGPT 80%; authoritativeness — legal AI avg 76% vs ChatGPT 70%. Thomson Reuters and LexisNexis did not opt in; vLex (Vincent AI) agreed then withdrew before publication — [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/10/vals-ais-latest-benchmark-finds-legal-and-general-ai-now-outperform-lawyers-in-legal-research-accuracy.html); [Legal Technology: "market leaders are absent"](https://legaltechnology.com/?p=97903)

**Market events relevant to positioning**
- [INDEPENDENT] Robin AI (contract AI, UK) failed to close a ~$50M round, cut about a third of staff, and was listed for distressed sale in late Oct 2025 — [Legal Technology](https://legaltechnology.com/robin-ai-listed-for-distressed-sale-nine-months-after-making-the-sunday-times-100-tech-list/); [Geek Law Blog](https://www.geeklawblog.com/2025/11/is-the-collapse-of-robin-ai-a-one-off-or-a-sign-of-a-legal-tech-ai-bubble.html); [Law360](https://www.law360.com/pulse/articles/2403945)

### Inferences
- "Misgrounded citation" is the industry's named failure mode — Proof-or-Silence's verbatim-quote check (quote must exist on cited page, then entailment check e.g. MiniCheck) is a direct, demonstrable answer to the Stanford finding. Pitch line: "Stanford found leading legal AI misgrounds citations; we refuse to answer unless the verbatim quote is found on the cited page."
- No incumbent publishes an abstention/"I don't know" rate or a calibrated precision-at-coverage number; reporting our own answer-rate vs. accuracy curve on a held-out gold set is a differentiator that judges can check.
- Incumbents withdrawing from Vals benchmarks suggests transparency on evaluation is itself a positioning lever for a small entrant.

### Gaps
- I did not find any independent evaluation of Harvey, Hebbia, AlphaSense, Glean, Spellbook, Kira/Litera or vLex Vincent specifically on hallucination or citation precision (Vals covers Harvey/CoCounsel/vLex on document tasks only).
- Could not verify via search how Harvey Vault or Hebbia Matrix render sources (verbatim highlight vs. document link); from general product knowledge both show clickable source citations, but this is unverified here.
- No independent benchmark found for regulatory (as opposed to case-law) Q&A, nor for any tool's abstention behaviour.

## 2. Regulatory change management / RegTech: obligation mapping, horizon scanning, change alerts, policy-gap analysis

### Takeaway
The RegTech category is consolidating fast (CUBE bought Thomson Reuters Regulatory Intelligence, Acin, Kodex AI and 4CRisk; Corlytics bought Clausematch; Archer bought Compliance.ai), and the common product shape is "horizon scanning -> obligation extraction -> map to internal policies/controls -> alert". The well-funded new entrant is Norm Ai (agents that encode regulations, $1.2B valuation July 2026). None of the sources describe answer-level verbatim-quote verification or abstention; their unit of value is the obligation/control map, not a provable Q&A answer.

### Inferences
- Proof-or-Silence's "impact alert" (new circular -> affected bank clauses) is the same pattern as Compliance.ai/AscentAI/Corlytics policy mapping — so it is table stakes for RegTech, not a unique claim. The differentiator to pitch is that each mapping is backed by verbatim quotes on both sides (RBI clause + bank clause) and a checkable conflict rationale.
- AscentAI's "obligation as the unit" and "side-by-side change comparison" are UX patterns worth copying: show old vs new circular text diff with the obligation highlighted.
- Norm Ai's "supervisory agents that audit other AI" aligns with our hash-chained audit log; framing our log as "AI supervision evidence for FREE-AI audits" ties to RBI's assurance pillar (see section 3).

### Gaps
- No public pricing found for CUBE, Corlytics, AscentAI, Compliance.ai/Archer, Regology, FiscalNote, Wolters Kluwer OneSumX or Thomson Reuters Regulatory Intelligence (all quote-based enterprise sales).

- Could not confirm whether any of these RegTech vendors cover RBI content natively (CUBE claims global coverage generally; not verified for RBI).

## 3. India: RBI/SEBI compliance tools, Indian legal AI, and RBI's FREE-AI framework

### Takeaway
India has (a) legal-research AI built on proprietary case-law corpora (SCC Online, Manupatra, CaseMine AMICUS, Lexlegis.ai) focused on judgments, not on RBI circular Q&A, and (b) compliance-workflow RegTech (TeamLease RegTrack, etc.) that tracks RBI obligations as checklists, not as cited answers. RBI mandated technology-based compliance tools for banks (deadline reported as June 2024) and in Aug 2025 published the FREE-AI framework (7 sutras, 6 pillars, 26 recommendations) emphasising explainability, auditability, incident reporting and board-approved AI policy — which a verify-or-abstain, audit-logged system maps onto well. Fake AI citations are now a recognised problem in Indian courts/tribunals.

### Cited Findings
**RBI regulation**
- [INDEPENDENT / primary] RBI released the FREE-AI (Framework for Responsible and Ethical Enablement of AI) committee report on 13 Aug 2025; committee constituted 26 Dec 2024; 26 actionable recommendations — [RBI press release](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=61018); [RBI report page](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationReportDetails.aspx?ID=1306)
- [Law-firm summary] Seven sutras: Trust is the Foundation; People First; Innovation over Restraint; Fairness and Equity; Accountability; Understandable by Design; Safety, Resilience and Sustainability. Six pillars: Infrastructure, Policy, Capacity (innovation enablement) and Governance, Protection, Assurance (risk mitigation) — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/rbi-committee-report-on-responsible-ai-in-the-financial-sector-free-ai-framework)
- [Law-firm summary] Recommendations include board-approved AI governance policy, AI risk-management aligned with RBI guidelines, AI impact assessment before new use cases, extending model risk management to AI with audits/validation, standardised AI incident reporting and drift monitoring, explainable/auditable models for credit, underwriting and customer service, telling customers when they deal with AI and giving them a way to challenge AI decisions, and an AI innovation sandbox — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/rbi-committee-report-on-responsible-ai-in-the-financial-sector-free-ai-framework); [KPMG India](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2025/08/rbi-free-ai-committee-report-on-framework-for-responsible-and-ethical-enablement-of-artificial-intelligence.html)
- [Press] Report also proposes shared data infrastructure and indigenous, financial-sector-specific AI models — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/rbi-ai-panel-calls-for-balancing-innovation-with-strong-risk-safeguards-125081301705_1.html)
- [VENDOR summary of RBI circular] RBI directed SCBs (excl. RRBs), SFBs and payments banks to adopt technology-based tools to monitor internal compliance; trade press puts the deadline at June 2024 — [TeamLease RegTech blog](https://teamleaseregtech.com/blogs/102/compliance-management-systems-made-mandatory-by-rbi-for-banks); [TimesTech](https://timestech.in/unveiling-the-future-exploring-surge-in-bank-spending-on-regtech-in-2024/)

### Inferences
- Gap we can claim: no Indian product found that answers questions over RBI master directions + a bank's own policies with page-level verbatim proof, abstention, as-of-date validity and policy-vs-RBI conflict detection. Indian legal AI is case-law-centric; Indian RegTech is checklist/workflow-centric.
- FREE-AI hooks for the pitch: "Understandable by Design" + "Accountability" sutras -> verbatim quote + refusal; Assurance pillar (audits, incident reporting) -> hash-chained audit log; consumer disclosure/challenge -> every answer is contestable because the quote and page are shown; indigenous/sector-specific models -> RBI-corpus-specific system.

## 5. Who explicitly does: verbatim quote + page highlight, abstain, cross-document contradiction detection, temporal/version awareness, audit trails

### Takeaway
Linked citations are universal; verified-citation checking exists (Lexis Shepard's Citation Agent, Manupatra Citation Verifier) but is about whether a case citation exists/is good law, not whether a quote supports the answer. Change-diffs and policy-to-regulation mapping exist in RegTech (AscentAI, Compliance.ai, Corlytics). I found no vendor that publicly claims explicit abstention ("I can't prove this, so I won't answer") or automated conflict detection between a firm's own policy and a regulator's text with quotes on both sides. That combination — plus as-of-date validity in the same Q&A answer — is the claimable gap.

**Features/UX patterns worth copying**
- Lexis: flag any citation that cannot be linked instead of silently showing it -> show a "could not verify" state distinct from abstain.
- Lexis Deep Research: authority table + counterarguments section -> for us, "governing provisions" table plus "conflicting/superseded provisions" section.
- AscentAI: side-by-side old vs new regulatory text with obligations highlighted; obligation as the atomic unit linked to policies/controls.
- Compliance.ai / Corlytics: auto-mapping of regulatory change to internal policies -> our impact alert, but show quotes on both sides.
- Luminance "Panel of Judges": multiple checkers must agree -> surface our verifier ensemble (string match + NLI/MiniCheck) as visible "proof checks passed: 3/3".
- Norm Ai "supervisory agents": an AI that audits AI -> brand our audit log as a supervisory record for FREE-AI assurance.
- Vals-style public scorecard: publish accuracy, citation precision and abstention rate on a gold set (incumbents withdrew from benchmarks — transparency is a wedge).

**Gaps we can credibly claim (based on what was and wasn't found)**
1. Answer-level proof, not just links: the quote must literally appear on the cited page and entail the answer — directly targets Stanford's "misgrounded" failure mode.
2. Explicit abstention as a feature with a measured abstain rate — no vendor publicly claims this.
3. Bank-policy-vs-RBI contradiction detection with dual quotes — RegTech maps obligations to policies but does not claim quote-level conflict explanation.
4. As-of-date validity in the answer itself ("valid as of DD-MM-YYYY; superseded by circular X") — RegTech does change alerts, Q&A tools don't claim temporal answers.
5. RBI-native, India-priced, and FREE-AI-aligned (explainable, auditable, contestable) — global tools lack RBI corpus; Indian tools lack verification depth.
6. Deterministic EMI/APR computation in code rather than LLM arithmetic (KFS compliance angle) — no competitor found claiming this for RBI KFS.
