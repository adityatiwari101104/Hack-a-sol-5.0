Tags: [EVIDENCE] = published rubric/template/official rule or a named winner list. [OPINION] = a judge's or blogger's advice. [CLASSIC] = pre-2022 material. [UNVERIFIED] = could not confirm.

## 1. Real winning decks / slides and their slide order

### Takeaway
No winning deck was retrievable. What is available is (a) official SIH idea-stage template (6 slides), (b) YC/Seibel and YC Demo Day structure, (c) Kawasaki's classic 10-slide order, (d) lists of winners with one-line descriptions (DTCC AI hackathon, OpenAI Open Model Hackathon). Use these as the evidence base for slide order, and treat anything claiming to be a "winner deck" as unverified.

### Cited Findings
- [EVIDENCE] SIH 2025/2026 official idea template, in order: (1) Title page (PS ID, title, theme, category, team ID/name); (2) Idea title / proposed solution (how it addresses the problem, innovation and uniqueness); (3) Technical approach (technologies, implementation methodology via flow charts, images or working prototype); (4) Feasibility and viability (challenges, risks, mitigations); (5) Impact and benefits (social, economic, environmental); (6) Research and references. Max six slides including title; points/diagrams/infographics not paragraphs; PDF only; do not alter the template's pointers. — [SlideShare SIH 2026 template](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/innovative-smart-india-hackathon-2026-idea-submission-template-for-software-hardware-solutions/289256695); same structure in the SIH 2025 Google Slides template [here](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1UAcsy0OUn86ZzrcGbXTdcyoc6uqV4Jr2/htmlpresent)
- [OPINION, unverified as winner] A GitHub "SIH 2025 Winner Format" generator recommends bullets only, max 6 bullets per slide, a system-architecture flowchart on the Technical Approach slide, and a 3-minute talk at roughly 30 s per slide. The repo has 0 stars, 1 fork and does not claim a win; "winner" only names the style. — [varuntejreddy03/sih2025](https://github.com/varuntejreddy03/sih2025)
- [UNVERIFIED] "SIH Winners Vault 2026" sells 8 winning PPTs; a sales page, no independent verification. — [superprofile listing](https://superprofile.bio/vp/sih-winners-vault-2026-%E2%80%93-ppts---source-code--2023-25-)
- [EVIDENCE, winners but no slides] DTCC's first AI-powered hackathon (2025) winners: Gold CIBC (centralized AI platform retrieving regulatory updates, claimed "months of work" saved per person per year, $100k FINOS grant); Accenture (vendor award; gen-AI tool reviewing regulatory documents, spotting changes and checking them against internal controls); Duke "Cipher Sentinel" (academic award, wallet monitoring); Silver Cyber ThirdEye; Bronze Xypher (Deutsche Bank). Page gives no judging criteria. Directly relevant: regulatory-document AI won gold and a vendor award. — [DTCC](https://www.dtcc.com/insights/2025/dtcc-announces-winners-from-first-ai-powered-hackathon)

- [CLASSIC / EVIDENCE of format] YC Demo Day structure (Geoff Ralston guide): intro (what you do, one sentence), problem, product and customer, opportunity (bottom-up market size), traction, team, business model, conclusion repeating the 3-4 key points. One point per slide, ~7 words or fewer, large fonts, high contrast, avoid complex/cumulative graphs, no videos, "live demos seldom work" so explain the product clearly, one presenter, practice repeatedly. — [YC Guide to Demo Day Pitches](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
- [CLASSIC / EVIDENCE of format] Seibel's seven answers: what do you do; how big is the market (bottom-up); progress relative to time spent; unique insight; business model; team (credentials only if highly relevant, e.g. regulated fields); the ask. "Email test": write two sentences, have a smart friend explain it back. "You don't want pizzazz." — [YC How to Pitch Your Company](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/)
- [CLASSIC] Kawasaki 10/20/30: 10 slides, 20 minutes, 30pt min font (page returned 403; partial secondary list: title, problem/opportunity, value proposition, competitive analysis, management team, timeline). — [search summary, Guy Kawasaki](https://www.guykawasaki.com/the-only-10-slides-you-need-in-your-pitch) (not fetched; treat details as secondary)

## 2. Published judging rubrics and weights

### Takeaway
Published rubrics cluster on technical complexity, originality/idea, practicality/working demo, usability/design, and impact; weights are rarely published, and MLH explicitly publishes none. Where weights exist (one small event) technical is largest at 40%.

### Cited Findings
- [EVIDENCE] MLH judging plan: science-fair style; 4 minutes per project (2 min presentation/demo, 1 min questions/scoring, 1 min travel); 3 rounds per project; judge briefing says "3 minutes per team" (the page is internally inconsistent); stack ranking, each judge picks top 3 (3/2/1 points); judges told to prioritize learning over commercial viability; teams submit 2-minute demo videos (demos, not presentations); cheating check on winners. No explicit criteria weights. — [MLH Organizer Guide](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/judging-and-submissions/judging-plan)
- [EVIDENCE] ETHGlobal criteria (five, no published weights): Technicality, Originality, Practicality (complete, functional, usable today), Usability (UI/UX/DX), WOW factor. Demo video 2-4 min, no speed-up, no TTS/AI voiceover, backstory under ~20 s, show the project in action. Finalists: 7 min = 4 min demo + 3 min Q&A; slides summarize key points, max 4 bullets each. — [ETHGlobal event details](https://ethglobal.com/events/unite/info/details)
- [EVIDENCE] Example of explicit weights from one event (Global Travel Hackathon): Technical 40% (demo-ready, data/APIs, technical challenges solved), Idea 30%, Design 20%, Wow factor 10%. Single small event; not representative. — [GitHub Evaluation-Criteria](https://github.com/Global-Travel-Hackathon/Evaluation-Criteria)
- [EVIDENCE of format] SIH idea-stage evaluation criteria: novelty, complexity, and clarity/detail of prescribed fields. — [IISER Kolkata idea-submission PDF](https://www.iiserkol.ac.in/~outreach/pdf/idea-submission-process.pdf) (via search snippet; not opened). Finale rubric NOT found. A college-level internal round listed pitching, PPT content, novelty, USPs, team technical strength. — search result, source vssut.ac.in doc (snippet only).
- [OPINION] Generic summary: most hackathons use 4-5 criteria (innovation, technical execution, impact, presentation, design); labels need definitions or judges read them differently. — [DoraHacks on dev.to](https://dev.to/dorahacks/how-to-design-a-hackathon-judging-plan-olf) (via search snippet)
- [EVIDENCE] OpenAI-hackathon Build Week 2026 published criteria cited by one article: technical implementation, design and UX, potential impact, quality of the idea (weights not published). — [dev.to](https://dev.to/jsb-securedme/what-ai-hackathon-winners-actually-have-in-common-1kla)
- Devpost's own judging doc was not found; Devpost events post criteria on the event page and one guide says 1-5 scoring per criterion. — search snippet only.

### Inferences
- Reasonable working weights for planning: working demo/practicality and technical depth ~35-40% combined, idea/originality ~20-25%, impact ~15-20%, clarity/design ~15-20%. This is a synthesis, not a published figure.

### Gaps
- Hackasol 5.0's own rubric was not searched; check the organizer brief. SIH finale rubric and Devpost official guide not found.

## 3. Playbooks: structure, timing, demo-first vs slides-first

### Takeaway
Multiple judges and organizers converge: land what it does in the first sentence, show a working demo early, keep slides minimal, rehearse the opening 30 seconds, hold a recorded backup, and respect the time limit.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION, judge, year of judging] Score is set in the first 30 seconds; scored factors: whether the demo ran, whether the opening sentence says what it is, scope fit, one-line identity judges can repeat. Failures: spending a third of the slot on context before the product, blurring what was built during the event, saying nothing breaks. Naming a known weakness ("what breaks first at scale") raised scores. Record a 60-second screen capture backup. Polish beyond legibility, framework choice, team credentials and slide decks did not move scores. No numeric weights. — [dev.to, Kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)
- [OPINION, organizer, 2014 so CLASSIC] Don't begin with a long PowerPoint, dive into the app; opening line states what the app does and repeat it at close; judges must be able to summarize in one sentence; state the intended user; land key points early; do not dwell on what failed; don't fake with JPEGs or claim unused tech; be ready to answer tech questions; test AV early. — [Microsoft Learn archive, Harrington](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/archive/blogs/matt-harrington/tips-for-winning-hackathons)
- [OPINION, 2026] JetBrains judging-table post: a strong project with a confusing demo loses to a simpler project judges understand; lead with the problem; rehearse aloud with a timer. (Page returned 403; claims are from search-result summary only.) — [JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)
- [OPINION] Other judge points (search snippets): "three minutes means three minutes, not five"; don't narrate struggles; many teams spend time on features not on how the solution solves the problem; simple frame problem, solution, predicted impact. — sources: [Bird](https://bird.com/blog/present-project-hackathon-judges), [Salesforce dev blog](https://developer.salesforce.com/blogs/2014/07/deliver-winning-pitch-hackathons)
- [OPINION] YC: live demos seldom work in Demo Day format; use one presenter chosen for presenting skill; speak slowly, face the audience. — [YC Demo Day guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
- [OPINION, conflicting] Demo-first: Microsoft/Harrington and Kurbaitaev say show the app immediately; YC says explain rather than live-demo (investor pitch context, not hackathon). SIH-style rubric wants a technical-approach/feasibility/impact slide structure. Reconcile: one-sentence opener, then demo, then a single architecture/metrics slide.
- Time per slide: only secondhand figure found is ~30 s/slide for six slides in 3 minutes (GitHub SIH generator, above). MLH judging itself allots 2 minutes to presentation+demo per judging round. [EVIDENCE of time pressure]

### Inferences
- With MLH-style 2-4 minute slots at tables, a 3-minute pitch should be about 60% demo/proof, 40% framing.

### Gaps
- No repeat-winner write-up with exact per-slide seconds was found; no verified data on live vs recorded success rates.

## 4. Metrics, architecture, impact; why teams lose

### Takeaway
Evidence favors: one before/after result, one architecture diagram, one named user story; losers have too much context, no working demo, overclaiming, hidden limits.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION] Strong entries state the problem, what existed before, what was improved, technical choices, and what users can do now; include README, architecture diagram, demo video, screenshots, known limitations; disclose honestly where AI helped; show tests, security, error handling, privacy, deployment readiness. — [dev.to, amising6](https://dev.to/amising6/what-i-learned-after-reviewing-many-ai-and-developer-projects-as-a-hackathon-judge-2g06)
- [EVIDENCE of standard] YC/Seibel: market size should be bottom-up (customers x price), not "share of a huge market"; progress shown relative to time spent. — [Seibel](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/); [YC Demo Day guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
- [OPINION] Judges penalize polished UI with shallow depth, strong engineering with thin docs, ambitious platforms without proof of usability, and claiming AI built everything (implied by contrasts in the article). — amising6 above
- [EVIDENCE of mechanism] Claims are checked against what was built: an AI-judging tool write-up says it can flag a "custom model" that is a wrapper or a README describing a backend absent from the repo. — [Devfolio](https://devfolio.co/blog/the-discerning-machine/) (search snippet only)
- [OPINION] Rubric-ignoring hurts: tailor the submission to the posted criteria. — Devpost-related guidance in search snippet (dev.to "make your Devpost submissions not suck")
- Typical SIH template instructions: diagrams over paragraphs, precise, unique idea. — SIH template above

### Inferences
- For a compliance assistant, the credible "metric" slide is: baseline (plain LLM / naive RAG) vs your system on a held-out set, with refusal/abstention rate and citation accuracy, plus one failure case shown honestly. This follows from the "name a weakness" and "evidence it works" findings; no source gave a compliance-specific example.

### Gaps
- No quantified study on slide text density vs scores; no source on "AI-generated-looking slides" specifically was verified.

## 5. AI/LLM-specific judging, 2024-2026

### Takeaway
Sources say judges reward: AI that is necessary to the solution, demo that shows behavior, honest limits and AI-use disclosure, real domain usefulness. Direct evidence about "ChatGPT wrapper fatigue" is thin.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION] Winners had "a necessary AI component, with a clear reason the model matters", visible execution with honest limits, obvious target user. Domain fine-tune won "Most Useful" because of real domain usefulness, not novelty. — [dev.to](https://dev.to/jsb-securedme/what-ai-hackathon-winners-actually-have-in-common-1kla)
- [OPINION] Stronger AI-era submissions are honest about AI help and don't claim AI built the whole project. — [amising6](https://dev.to/amising6/what-i-learned-after-reviewing-many-ai-and-developer-projects-as-a-hackathon-judge-2g06)
- [OPINION] Focused scope impresses more than "we tried to build everything." Three features and no center don't survive judges' recall. — [Kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)
- [EVIDENCE] Gold at DTCC AI hackathon went to a regulatory-update retrieval platform framed around time saved. — [DTCC](https://www.dtcc.com/insights/2025/dtcc-announces-winners-from-first-ai-powered-hackathon)
- [OPINION] Judge criteria (Bolt hackathon): clear purpose, thoughtful execution, real usefulness; "rough around the edges" is acceptable if shipped; originality, not copies of existing products. — [Bolt judge spotlight](https://bolt.new/blog/hackathon-judge-spotlight)

### Inferences
- Judges' "wrapper" skepticism implies a zero-guessing/abstention design, evals against a baseline, and source citations are exactly the differentiators; but no source here explicitly quantifies judge fatigue.

### Gaps
- No published judge statement specifically on "ChatGPT wrapper" fatigue was verified; Devfolio snippet is the closest. Cost/baseline reporting as a judged factor: no source.

## Recommended 3-minute structure (180 s)

| # | Slide / segment | Seconds | Purpose | Evidence behind it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One-sentence opener plus named user (urban co-op bank compliance officer), concrete pain (missed/misread RBI circular) | 0-20 | State what it is and for whom; set the score in first 30 s | Kurbaitaev; Harrington #6, #8, #9; Seibel "what do you do"; YC Demo Day intro |
| 2 | Live demo (or recorded fallback) of one end-to-end question: answer with citation, then a question it refuses to answer | 20-85 | Prove it works; show zero-guessing behavior | Kurbaitaev (demo ran = top factor); Harrington #5; ETHGlobal "Practicality"; MLH demos encouraged |
| 3 | Architecture: one diagram (ingest, retrieve, verify, abstain, audit) | 85-110 | Technical depth, credibility | SIH Technical Approach slide with flow chart; ETHGlobal "Technicality"; GitHub SIH format advice |
| 4 | Proof: baseline vs system on held-out set (accuracy, citation validity, refusal on unanswerable), plus one honest failure/limit and cost | 110-145 | Evals, baselines, honest limits for LLM credibility | amising6 (honest limits); Kurbaitaev ("what breaks first"); inference for compliance metrics |
| 5 | Impact and feasibility: one concrete user story (hours saved per circular, audit trail), bottom-up count of co-op banks, deployment/privacy (on-prem, PII masking) | 145-170 | Impact, feasibility | SIH Feasibility and Impact slides; Seibel bottom-up market; DTCC "time saved" framing |
| 6 | Closing: repeat one-line identity, team, ask (pilot with a UCB / prize) | 170-180 | Recall for deliberation; ask | Harrington #6, #8; Seibel "what do you want"; YC conclusion |

Keep to 3 or 4 content slides plus the demo; max 4 bullets/slide (ETHGlobal), ideally images/diagrams (SIH, YC). If the SIH-style template is mandated, map: slide 1 problem+solution, 2 technical approach, 3 feasibility, 4 impact, but still lead with the demo in the spoken pitch.

## Top 10 judge-reward signals (source-backed)

1. Working, end-to-end demo that runs (Kurbaitaev; Harrington; ETHGlobal Practicality).
2. Opening sentence says what it is and for whom (Kurbaitaev; Harrington; Seibel).
3. One-line identity judges can repeat during deliberation (Kurbaitaev; Harrington #8).
4. Narrow, finished scope over sprawling features (Kurbaitaev; amising6).
5. Real, specific user and real domain problem (Harrington #9; OpenAI-hackathon winners analysis; Bolt judges).
6. Technical depth that is visible: architecture diagram, hard problems solved (ETHGlobal Technicality; Global Travel 40% technical; SIH Technical Approach).
7. Honest limits and a named failure mode (Kurbaitaev; amising6; dev.to winners analysis).
8. Evidence it works: before/after or baseline comparison, tests, docs (amising6; SIH feasibility; YC "progress").
9. AI that is necessary, with honest disclosure of AI use; claims match the repo (dev.to winners; amising6; Devfolio snippet).
10. Time discipline and clarity: 3 minutes means 3 minutes, minimal text, rehearsed opening (Bird snippet; YC Demo Day guide; ETHGlobal 4-bullet rule; JetBrains snippet).

Caveats: most items are single-judge opinion blogs; items 1-4 and 10 recur across independent sources. Several citations rely on search snippets rather than fully fetched pages, noted inline.
