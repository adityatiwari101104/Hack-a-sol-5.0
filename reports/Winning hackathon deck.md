# Win judges with proof, not polish

Build the eight slides around one demonstrated behaviour, "it refuses to guess", and let a real screenshot, one architecture diagram and one honest baseline comparison carry the technical argument. The evidence for this is consistent but thin. I could not retrieve a single verifiably winning SIH, Devpost, Hack-a-sol or Inter IIT deck, so slide order below comes from official templates, published rubrics and judge write-ups, not from inspected winners. Three signals recur across independent sources: judges decide in the first 30 seconds, a demo that actually runs is the top scored factor, and naming a limit honestly raises scores ([Kurbaitaev, dev.to](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)). The design research is firmer: a sentence headline plus one visual beats topic-and-bullets for complex ideas and delayed recall ([Penn State assertion-evidence research](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/assertion-evidence-slides-appear-to-lead-to-better-comprehension--2/)), which is what a judge needs after twenty-plus teams. The product's existing tokens (paper, slate, one ink-blue accent) are already the right structure for projection, but four state colours fail colour-blind or contrast checks as shipped and need the small changes listed in the palette table. Draw every diagram as native PowerPoint shapes from one layout function; do not paste images. Two things are unverified and should be checked early: Hackasol 5.0's own rubric and slot length, and the palette on the venue projector.

## What winners and judges actually reward

No winning deck could be opened. Searches returned blank templates, format generators and a paid "winners vault" sales page, which I treat as unverified ([superprofile listing](https://superprofile.bio/vp/sih-winners-vault-2026-%E2%80%93-ppts---source-code--2023-25-)). What exists is structural evidence. The official SIH idea template is six slides: title, proposed solution and innovation, technical approach with flow chart, feasibility and risks, impact and benefits, research and references, with diagrams preferred over paragraphs ([SIH 2026 template](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/innovative-smart-india-hackathon-2026-idea-submission-template-for-software-hardware-solutions/289256695)). Y Combinator's Demo Day format runs intro, problem, product, opportunity, traction, team, business model and a closing repeat of the key points, one point per slide in about seven words, and warns that live demos seldom work in that setting ([YC Demo Day guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)). A GitHub SIH "winner format" generator suggests about 30 seconds per slide and at most six bullets, but the repo has no stars and does not claim a win, so it is opinion ([varuntejreddy03/sih2025](https://github.com/varuntejreddy03/sih2025)).

The closest winner evidence for this domain is DTCC's 2025 AI hackathon. Gold went to CIBC for a platform that retrieves regulatory updates and was framed around months of work saved per person per year, and Accenture took a vendor award for a tool that reviews regulatory documents, spots changes and checks them against internal controls ([DTCC](https://www.dtcc.com/insights/2025/dtcc-announces-winners-from-first-ai-powered-hackathon)). That page publishes no slides or criteria, so the lesson is only that regulatory-document AI pitched on outcomes can win. Likewise, an analysis of the OpenAI Open Model Hackathon says a domain fine-tune won "Most Useful" for real-domain usefulness, not novelty, though the pattern claims are the author's inference ([dev.to](https://dev.to/jsb-securedme/what-ai-hackathon-winners-actually-have-in-common-1kla)).

Published rubrics cluster on five themes. ETHGlobal scores technicality, originality, practicality, usability and wow factor, with no weights, and asks finalists for slides with at most four bullets each ([ETHGlobal](https://ethglobal.com/events/unite/info/details)). MLH publishes no weights and runs roughly two minutes of presentation and demo per judging round, with judges told to prioritise learning over commercial viability ([MLH judging plan](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/judging-and-submissions/judging-plan)). The only explicit weights I found are from one small event, technical 40%, idea 30%, design 20%, wow 10% ([Global Travel Hackathon](https://github.com/Global-Travel-Hackathon/Evaluation-Criteria)); treat that as unrepresentative. A reasonable planning split, which is my synthesis and not a published figure, is demo and technical depth 35-40%, idea 20-25%, impact 15-20% and clarity 15-20%.

Judge write-ups agree on process. Kurbaitaev reports the score is set in the first 30 seconds, that failure comes from spending a third of the slot on context, and that naming "what breaks first at scale" raised scores while polish beyond legibility did not ([dev.to](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)). A Microsoft organiser's older guide says to open with what the app does, state the intended user, and not fake with images ([Microsoft Learn archive](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/archive/blogs/matt-harrington/tips-for-winning-hackathons)). Another judge says strong entries disclose AI use, show tests and privacy handling, and are penalised for polished UI with shallow depth ([amising6, dev.to](https://dev.to/amising6/what-i-learned-after-reviewing-many-ai-and-developer-projects-as-a-hackathon-judge-2g06)). There is a real conflict worth stating: demo-first advice comes from hackathon judges, while YC's "explain, don't live-demo" advice is for investor stages. For a hackathon table, the demo-first camp is the better fit, with a recorded fallback ([Reskilll](https://blogs.reskilll.com/hackathon-demo-presentation-tips-pitch-3-minutes-win-2026/)).

For an AI entry specifically, the sources say judges want a necessary AI component, visible behaviour, honest limits and a clear user. I found no verified judge statement on "ChatGPT wrapper" fatigue, so the argument that abstention, citations and a baseline eval are the differentiators is inference, though a plausible one. The team's edge is that "zero guessing" is a behaviour that can be shown in one screenshot: an answer with a source span next to a question the system refuses.

## An eight-slide order that fits a judge's attention

Because slot length is unknown (the sources assume two to four minutes), I plan for three minutes of speaking and let the extra slides serve as demo bookends and an appendix-style backup. If the slot is longer, expand the demo, not the slide count. The order below maps onto the SIH template that Indian judges are primed for, but leads with the problem and demo as the judge-write-ups advise.

| # | Slide (headline is a full sentence) | Seconds | Evidence it earns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name the user and the one-line identity: a co-op bank compliance officer who cannot afford a guessed answer | 0-20 | First-30-second score; Seibel "what do you do" |
| 2 | The problem as a concrete miss: one real RBI circular, one wrong plain-LLM answer | 20-40 | Problem stated fast; SIH problem slide |
| 3 | Real product screenshot, three numbered callouts: answer, source span, withheld sentence | 40-75 | Demo ran; ETHGlobal practicality |
| 4 | Plain RAG vs ours: one lane with the verify gate inserted | 75-100 | Technical depth; SIH technical approach |
| 5 | The verification ladder: five code checks, cheap models first, any fail withheld with reason | 100-120 | Visible hard problem solved |
| 6 | Proof: baseline vs system on held-out set, n and interval printed, one honest failure | 120-150 | Evidence and named weakness |
| 7 | Amendment chain and contradiction: the as-of date picks the clause; bank vs RBI conflict | 150-170 | Domain specificity (the differentiator) |
| 8 | Impact, feasibility, privacy, ask: pilot with a co-op bank; repeat the one-line identity | 170-180 | SIH feasibility and impact; closing repeat |

The live demo sits between slides 3 and 4; slide 3's crop must equal the demo's starting screen so the switch feels continuous, and a recorded 60-second capture is the fallback. A caution on slide 8: YC and Seibel favour a bottom-up market count (customers times price) over "share of a huge market" ([Seibel](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/)), so count co-operative banks you could actually reach, with a source, or leave the number out.

## Slide design that survives a projector and a glance

The best-supported rule is a sentence headline stating the claim plus one visual as evidence. In a study of 110 engineering students, the assertion-evidence group showed better comprehension, fewer misconceptions, lower perceived load and stronger delayed recall ([Penn State](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/how-the-design-of-presentation-slides-affects-audience-comprehens/)); it did not help short-term recall of simple facts ([ASEE](https://peer.asee.org/17510)), so the one number you want remembered should stand alone and large. Duarte's glance test, grasping a slide in three seconds or less, is the practical check ([Duarte](https://www.duarte.com/resources/slide-glance-test/)). Mayer's multimedia principles back the same direction: removing decoration has the strongest effect and labels placed next to what they describe also help, while duplicating narration verbatim on screen is the weakest effect (published effect sizes differ by source, so I do not quote them) ([Mayer summary](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf)). Every numeric word limit is practitioner heuristic, not research; my defaults are headline up to 15 words, at most about 30 words on a slide, and no more than seven visible objects.

On type, practitioner guidance puts body text at a 24 pt floor and 28-32 pt as safe ([UF/IFAS](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/WC104/pdf); [BrightCarbon](https://www.brightcarbon.com/blog/presentation-font-size)). A workable scale on a 13.33 by 7.5 inch slide is headline 36-44 pt, hero number 120-200, body 24-28, chart labels at least 20, and source or n lines 14-18, with any caveat that matters at 18 or more. Tufte's Columbia analysis is the cautionary tale: a key finding buried in small type on a bullet-heavy slide was cited by the accident board ([Tufte](https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/the-columbia-evidence/)), which maps directly to never hiding n or a limit in a grey footer.

Data slides should be one chart with the finding as the title. Use at most three bars (plain LLM, plain RAG, ours), our bar in the single accent and baselines grey, direct labels instead of a legend ([Storytelling with Data](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2012/06/drawing-attention-with-data-labels)), the axis from zero, and the n, split and interval printed under the chart. Bars with error bars convey uncertainty poorly ([Correll and Gleicher](https://vis.mit.edu/classes/6.859/readings/pdfs/Corell-ErrorBarsConsideredHarmful.pdf)), so with a small gold set show the per-item dots or state the interval in words.

For comparison, advice on feature grids is split: some guides prefer a capability table, others call the ten-row tick grid a round-killer ([Papermark](https://www.papermark.com/blog/competitor-analysis-pitch-deck); [WaveUp](https://waveup.com/blog/how-to-make-a-winning-competition-slide-for-your-pitch-deck/)). Both agree that credibility comes from including a row you lose. I recommend skipping the grid and showing a same-input before/after: left, the plain-LLM answer, fluent and uncited with one wrong claim marked; right, ours, each claim with a source span and one sentence withheld. Same document, same crop size, so the eye does the diff. If a matrix is demanded, keep it to four by four with text values and one row where a competitor wins.

For the live product, use the documentation-style callout recipe: crop tight, at most three numbered callouts, text outside the UI joined by leader lines, one colour system, a zoom inset for tiny elements, and callouts built one per click with the full annotated state as the last frame ([Dynatrace style guide](https://styleguide.dynatrace.com/docs/product-capture/screenshot-annotations/); [Zight](https://zight.com/blog/how-to-annotate-screenshots-for-clear-instructions)). Fill 55-65% of the slide with the screenshot, capture at 1920 wide with UI zoom 125-150%, use demo-corpus data only, and skip device mockups. Reported tells of template-made decks are gradients, generic icon sets and copy that could belong to any startup ([Single Grain](https://www.singlegrain.com/blog-posts/ai-pitch-decks-generic/); [2Slides](https://2slides.com/blog/why-ai-slides-look-fake-and-how-to-fix)); these sources are vendors, so treat them as opinion. The counter-move is specificity: real screenshots on at least three slides, varied layouts, and one real failure case from your own eval.

## A colour system that survives a lit room and colour-blind viewers

The product's structure (paper, two slates, one ink-blue accent, no gradients) already suits slides. Keep roughly 60% paper, 30% ink and 10% accent, ration the state colours so each appears only on the slide about that state, and keep large fills low-chroma while saturated colour stays on small marks (this proportioning is my reasoning; I found no primary source for 60-30-10).

Contrast needs margin beyond WCAG. WCAG 2.2 asks for 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)), but projector sources report that even about 2 foot-candles of ambient light can pull a front-projected image below 10:1 ([Live Design](https://www.livedesignonline.com/display-killer-projected-images), seen only as a search snippet). No source gives a projector-specific target, so the thresholds here are derived from a simple veiling-light model in the research notes: body text at least 7:1, state words and bold labels of 24 pt or more at least 5.5:1, non-text marks at least 4.5:1 and 2 px or thicker, and hairlines as decoration only. APCA, an alternative contrast model, gives a second opinion: ink Lc 98, ink-2 Lc 84, state colours Lc 74-86, which clears its 45 floor for large headlines but sits just under its 75 body-text floor, consistent with using state colours as large bold labels only ([APCA](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)). Use light body slides, and dark only for title and close, since a dark field under ambient light renders as grey and WCAG overstates dark-colour contrast (the polarity advice is heuristic).

Colour-blind safety is where the shipped tokens fail. Okabe and Ito advise never conveying information by colour alone, using thick marks, and varying brightness not only hue ([Okabe and Ito](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/)); Tol warns about yellow/green and blue/violet pairings ([Paul Tol](https://sronpersonalpages.nl/~pault/)). The team's own simulation (CIEDE2000 on Machado 2009 matrices, entered from memory and not cross-checked, so figures are approximate within a few units) found the product's proven green and withheld red-brown nearly collide for deuteranopes at ΔE 6.6 (about 10 or more is comfortable, my rule of thumb) and protanopes at 8.8, and the conflict magenta and abstain blue at 6.9 for protanopes. The fixes below raise those to 13.9 and 16.2, and 11.1. Two residual collisions cannot be fixed by hue: conflict versus superseded grey under deuteranopia (ΔE 1.5) and abstain versus the accent blue. So every state also carries a printed word and a glyph: check for proven, cross and struck text for withheld, not-equal sign and two columns for conflict, hollow pip and strike-through or hatching for superseded, and open circle plus "ABSTAIN" on a slate card for abstain. Bank versus RBI is safe (ΔE above 46 for all simulated types) but should still be solid purple versus dashed orange with words.

State colours follow the legal-citator convention where it helps. KeyCite uses red for "no longer good law" or superseded and yellow for caution; Shepard's uses red, yellow and a blue neutral signal ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/drafting-assistant/westcheck/keycite-status-flags.html); [Cincinnati law library](https://www.law.uc.edu/academics-career/library/marx-markings-blog/2025-blog-archives/understanding-keycite-and-shepard-s-symbols.html)). The product maps proven to green, withheld to red-brown, conflict to a distinct magenta that cannot be misread as stop or caution, superseded to grey with strike-through, and abstain to calm slate blue, never red. Keep amber solely as the evidence-quote highlighter so no one reads it as a warning.

For Indian banking, blue is the safe family: SBI and HDFC both use it, but I found no official hex values for RBI, SBI, HDFC, ICICI or Kotak, and third-party sites disagree, so do not imitate any brand colour or RBI's emblem. Avoid saffron-white-green in sequence (reads as the flag or a party), large saffron or orange fields, and red as a "success" colour, since red signals both danger and auspiciousness ([GKToday](https://www.gktoday.in/colour-symbolism-in-indian-culture-festivals-and-national-identity/); [World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/stories/geographies-in-depth/the-secret-history-of-indias-flag/)). The RBI orange stays a thin line and a word, never a field.

## Diagrams that read in five seconds and stay editable

Treat each diagram as a billboard with one message stated as the slide title. The sourced basis is Duarte's three-second test ([Duarte](https://www.duarte.com/resources/guides-tools/the-glance-test/)), working memory of about four chunks ([Cowan 2001](https://memory.psych.missouri.edu/assets/doc/articles/2001/cowan-bbs-2001.pdf)), and C4's context view that shows one system in the centre with no technology detail ([c4model.com](https://c4model.com/diagrams/system-context)). The numeric caps are my inference: four to six top-level shapes, eight at most, strictly left to right, no crossings, one highlighted path in the accent colour at 4-6 pt with everything else grey at 1.5 pt, nouns of one to three words, and node text at least 24 px. Anthropic's agents article is a good public model of one pattern per figure, building from simple to complex, with a "gate" element between steps ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)); I could read only its text, not the figures.

Progressive disclosure should be "same diagram, one part lit" across consecutive slides, with identical coordinates and only the highlight changing, because in-slide animation breaks in PDF export, Google Slides import and offline judge review (this is design reasoning; I found no empirical comparison). Generate each from a single `draw(highlight)` function so positions never drift. Embedded fonts are also unreliable in Keynote and Google Slides ([iSpring](https://www.ispringsolutions.com/blog/how-to-embed-fonts-in-powerpoint)), so ship a PDF alongside the PPTX.

The five diagrams the deck needs:

| Diagram | Layout | Caps and colour roles |
|---|---|---|
| Plain RAG vs ours | Two stacked lanes in identical positions; the top lane (question, retrieve, answer, shown) is grey with a small "no check" tag; the bottom adds a large "code checks every sentence" gate, with verified and withheld outputs | Gate is the hero navy; LLM draft box dashed outline; at most six shapes in the lit lane |
| Verification ladder | One horizontal row of five rungs (ids exist, in force, quote and page, numbers, supported?); rung five holds three pills (HHEM, MiniCheck, LLM judge, "cheap first"); one red rail below: "any fail: withheld with reason" | Code rungs solid navy; local models cool tint; LLM judge outlined; about 45 words total |
| Amendment chain and as-of timeline | Horizontal axis, three version cards, one vertical date cursor; two stacked copies with cursor at 2025-09 and 2025-10 show "shall" fading to "may, at its option" | In-force card solid, superseded cards faded with strike; only the changed words highlighted; the EMI example is the team's and unverified by me |
| Contradiction side by side | Two equal cards facing each other with header chips (source, page, as-of date), deciding phrases underlined, and one verdict bar ("Conflict: RBI rule overrides bank clause") | Bank solid purple, RBI dashed orange; at most 70 words and quotes at 22 px or larger |
| Results bars | Grouped bars, baseline grey vs ours accent, direct labels, y from zero, n under the chart | Native editable chart, three or four groups |

The as-of timeline has a real-world precedent that judges may recognise. legislation.gov.uk shows point-in-time views with "latest available" and "as enacted" versions and flags superseded provisions, and eCFR offers a date field and compare-dates function; I saw these only via search descriptions and help pages, not a rendered page ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/16/schedule/8/part/4/2024-01-01?timeline=false); [eCFR reader aids](https://www.ecfr.gov/reader-aids)). Citing that familiarity shortens the explanation.

On tooling, build diagrams as native shapes from code. If the team is on Python, python-pptx offers real connectors with `begin_connect` and `end_connect` glue for rectangles, so arrows follow boxes when someone drags them in PowerPoint, though arrowheads need a small XML patch via a private API ([python-pptx connector analysis](https://python-pptx.readthedocs.io/en/latest/dev/analysis/shp-connector.html)). If on Node, official PptxGenJS supports arrowheads on line shapes (`beginArrowType` and `endArrowType`, since 3.3.0) and native charts, but no glued connectors, so generate arrow coordinates from the same layout function as the boxes ([PptxGenJS types](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gitbrent/PptxGenJS/master/types/index.d.ts)); a fork adds a connector API but carries maintenance risk ([shbernal fork](https://shbernal.github.io/PptxGenJS/reference/api/index/interfaces/ConnectorProps)). I did not test arrowhead rendering in current PowerPoint, so render one test slide first. Avoid SVG import for the final deck: "Convert to Shape" works only in Microsoft 365 for Windows ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/topic/69f29d39-194a-4072-8c35-dbe5e7ea528c)), Mermaid SVG labels use `foreignObject` that many consumers drop ([mermaid-cli issue](https://www.github.com/mermaid-js/mermaid-cli/issues/691)), and PNG blurs on projectors. Use Mermaid or draw.io for sketching only. I did not verify how Excalidraw, Figma, Lucidchart or Napkin exports behave in PowerPoint.

## Recommendations for the Proof-or-Silence deck

Build eight slides in the order given above and treat the screenshot slide and the abstention moment as the centre of the deck, with the demo run live between slides 3 and 4 and a 60-second recording ready. Write every headline as a claim, for example "Every sentence is checked by code before you see it", and keep each slide to about 30 words, one idea and no bullets as the main structure. Spend the visual budget on three things: a real annotated screenshot with at most three callouts, a same-input plain-LLM versus ours before/after, and the one-lane "gate inserted" pipeline diagram. Put a baseline you actually ran, named, on the proof slide, show n and the interval under the chart, and show one failure from your own eval in readable type; this is the strongest honest-limits signal available and is what judges said raised scores.

Adopt the changed palette values in the table, enforce word plus glyph plus line style for every state, keep state colours to the slide that is about them, and use dark only for title and close. Before the event, do a 30-second back-row test of paper, ink-2 and the five state labels on the venue projector, and thicken borders to 2 px if tints vanish. Do not name or imitate any bank's or RBI's brand colours. Fix four open items first: confirm Hackasol's slot length and rubric (not searched); check state-coloured text on the highlighter (only ink, ink-2 and accent were verified); re-verify contrast and colour-blind numbers with a second tool; and confirm the Plex weight, since Regular at 24 pt is borderline and Medium or SemiBold is safer. Notes per slide should carry the takeaway sentence, seconds, the transition cue, the click cue and a fallback line, each under about 60 words.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "how do we look polished" to "how fast can a judge verify our claim". The evidence says polish beyond legibility does not move scores, whereas a demo that runs, a one-line identity and a named limit do, and the same discipline applies to colour: in this deck, colour is a verification aid, so each state must be readable with colour removed. The product's core behaviour, withholding an unsupported sentence, is unusually easy to show in a single frame, which is a structural advantage over teams who must describe their value.

The main uncertainty is not design but evidence: no real winning deck was inspected, most judge advice is single-author opinion, and the colour numbers rest on an unverified in-house script and on-screen models, not a projector test. Treat the slide order and palette as strong defaults to be validated by one dry run in the venue with a timer, and let that rehearsal, not further reading, decide the remaining choices.
